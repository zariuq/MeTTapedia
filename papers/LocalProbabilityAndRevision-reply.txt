I agree that revision needs a probabilistic interpretation of how the quantities and observations belong together. The distinction I would make is between a globally coherent semantics and a single largest model that the implementation stores or evaluates.

For example, two fair-bit marginals admit both an equal-bit coupling and an opposite-bit coupling. After observing the second bit as true, the first is respectively certainly true or certainly false. Neither the marginal truth values nor distinct provenance IDs select the answer. A revision operation must retain or request the relevant joint law, a justified independence assumption, or a set of admissible couplings.

But the contexts can have no top. All finite subsets of an infinite variable set are directed by union and have no largest member. A compatible family of finite laws can still define an abstract global probability measure under the usual extension conditions. So “no largest working context” does not remove F's coherence requirement; it gives an implementation of it through local models.

Ben's three-pair example is a useful obstruction: each pair is valid and all one-variable overlaps agree, but the three pairs cannot be marginals of one classical joint law. This does not establish that such joint semantics is unnecessary. It means those particular assessments cannot all be treated as describing the same jointly meaningful classical variables. The system should report incompatibility, revise the assessments, change the context identifications, or restrict which joint questions it claims to answer.

A prior over probabilistic programs is a useful hierarchical-model construction once their observable meanings and normalization are specified. Evidence revises both their internal states and their model weights. A Bernoulli parameter alone does not provide an STV's confidence, and a transformer conditional distribution needs a declared truth-event and evidence interpretation before serving as a truth-value interface.

The proposed common contract is therefore: every admitted revision has an explicit context, joint query–evidence semantics, observation identity and dependency assumptions; larger compatible contexts preserve answers to the same query under the same evidence. No greatest context is required. Unrestricted classical probabilistic claims require the corresponding global compatibility; local/contextual claims must say where joint extension is unavailable.

MeTTaProN v0.3 already makes much of this distinction in Rules 10.1–10.3 and Section 17. Rule 17.1 should explicitly connect evidence multiplication to a joint observation kernel or conditional-independence/chain-rule certificate. The omega-PLN fidelity manuscript itself assumes a gluable covering context for its global-Bayes comparison. A covering context need not be the “largest” one.
