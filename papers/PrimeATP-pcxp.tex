\begin{abstract}
We test whether \Prime{} can both find proofs itself and independently check
proofs found by other theorem provers.  The test uses the
implication/negation frontier that motivated Nil Geisweiller's public \pcxp{}
chaining experiments.  The executable corpus is regenerated directly
from a pinned, CC0 \texttt{set.mm} (the Metamath Proof Explorer's database of
machine-checked mathematics): 182 provability assertions from
\texttt{ax-mp} through \texttt{ja}, with active essential hypotheses and
source order preserved.  On the full prior theory, Prime's guided
inhabitation search independently proves frontier theorems 181 and 183, but
does not prove theorem 182 at the tested bounds.  A chronological selector,
trained only on the source prefix preceding each target, reduces the three
frontier theories to 11, 11, and 12 assertions; Prime then discovers and
rechecks all three proofs in 0.10 seconds.  E and Vampire prove all twelve
corresponding first-order encodings.  Prime also replays
their actual derivations: an E proof of theorem 182 becomes twelve independently
checked binary-resolution steps and a direct proof term composed from eight
earlier rules, which Prime's ordinary type checker accepts.  E rewriting and
Vampire ordered superposition also replay against an independently checked
equality certificate.  A quantified extension checks the authentic
\texttt{set.mm} theorem \texttt{ax5d}, which states
$\varphi\to(\psi\to\forall x\,\psi)$ under the distinct-variable (DV)
condition that $x$ stays apart from $\psi$: with DV obligations hidden from
the producer, Prime rediscovers the constructive core \texttt{(a1i ax-5)}
within a two-assertion selected theory, then a source-level replayer infers
the substitutions and recomputes the required \texttt{\$d x ps} condition.
Nine destructive source-certificate mutations and one searched-term mutation
are rejected.

The experiment also exposed two correctness bugs: a cyclic substitution
accepted by an internal search unifier, and an unsound tautology test that
confused unifiability with literal identity.  Both fixes have positive and
falsifying regression tests, and the complete Prime test suite passes.  Prime
remains substantially behind mature automated theorem provers in full-theory
search.  The results separate established capabilities from remaining work:
proof checking and interchange succeed; small native resolution works; direct
search has useful classical pruning; full-theory saturation still needs the
standard redundancy and scheduling machinery that makes an ATP an ATP.
\end{abstract}

\section{What we built and why it matters}

We turned one concrete Metamath frontier into a controlled comparison of
direct proof search, established first-order automated theorem provers (ATPs),
and independent proof replay.  For each target theorem, the available library
is every earlier
provability assertion in \texttt{set.mm} together with the target's active
hypotheses.  Prime attempts to construct a typed proof term from that full
library.  E and Vampire receive first-order encodings generated from the same
statements.  Their successful derivations are then translated into explicit
resolution or ordered-equality steps and replayed by Prime.

This setup answers four different questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Can Prime discover direct proofs when the useful lemmas are mixed into a
  realistically growing theory?
\item When its native search fails, can Prime validate a proof found by a
  mature ATP without trusting that prover's success status?
\item Does the failure come from the theorem, the available rules, the proof
  calculus, or the search policy?
\item Can a selector discover a small useful theory from prior proof history
  without consulting the target's stored proof?
\end{enumerate}

The headline result is sharp.  Prime's full-theory search proves
theorems 181 and 183, while theorem 182 remains incomplete at the tested
bounds.  Chronological premise selection closes that gap: Prime searches and
rechecks all three frontier proofs from small theories chosen solely from each
earlier source prefix.  E and Vampire solve all twelve generated comparison
problems---four encodings for each of the three targets.  For theorem 182,
Prime also replays E's twelve-step resolution derivation and checks the
resulting direct proof term built from eight earlier rules.  Thus premise
selection, proof interchange, and checking all work across the frontier;
unguided full-library scaling remains the bottleneck.

The experiment grows out of Geisweiller's public \pcxp{} work on rediscovering
Metamath proofs and controlling backward search as the available theory
expands~\cite{geisweiller-chaining,geisweiller-ic,backward-via-forward}.
Bitseat's attention-allocation experiments provide a complementary
selected-theory setting~\cite{bitseat-aa}.  The benchmark itself is generated
from the CC0 \texttt{set.mm} source~\cite{setmm}, the single database from
which the Metamath Proof Explorer machine-checks mathematics from
propositional logic through ZFC set theory; the present frontier lies in its
opening implication/negation segment.

The implementation keeps three jobs separate:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c}
goal + exact theory\\[2pt]
$\downarrow$\\[-2pt]
\fbox{untrusted producer: inhabitation, native resolution, E, or Vampire}\\[2pt]
$\downarrow$ proof term or derivation certificate\\[-2pt]
\fbox{Prime replay + ordinary type checking}\\[2pt]
$\downarrow$\\[-2pt]
checked theorem or explicit rejection
\end{tabular}

Search may be heuristic, learned, incomplete, or simply wrong.  It does not
acquire theorem authority by being fast.  Conversely, the checker need not
implement the search strategy.  This boundary lets us compare calculi and
policies without weakening acceptance to accommodate any of them.

\section{The \texttt{set.mm} frontier benchmark}

The generator reads a pinned \texttt{set.mm} at revision
\texttt{47e6e06b}.  Every generated problem and its manifest carry the exact
source identity:

\begin{lstlisting}
revision = 47e6e06b87581cd630d210dc41cf83b02eea78ea
sha256   = 3aecffcfcab6f6e114cce1d873a8300d7f41f24928648c04164aaa305b1f491a
\end{lstlisting}

It parses the implication/negation grammar, tracks Metamath scopes and active
essential hypotheses, and extracts the exact provability sequence from
\texttt{ax-mp} to \texttt{ja}.  Two syntax constructors precede the historical
proof labels, so the resulting 182 assertions occupy indices 2--183.  Anchor
checks fail generation unless indices 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 181, 182, and 183 are
respectively \texttt{ax-mp}, \texttt{ax-1}, \texttt{ax-2}, \texttt{ax-3},
\texttt{mp2}, \texttt{pm2.61nii}, \texttt{pm2.61iii}, and \texttt{ja}.

For a theorem at index $i$, the \emph{full prior theory} contains exactly every
provability assertion at an index below $i$, plus the theorem's grounded
essential hypotheses.  The generator also emits two controls:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{core}: only \texttt{ax-mp}, \texttt{ax-1}, \texttt{ax-2}, and
  \texttt{ax-3}---modus ponens plus the three axiom schemes
  $\varphi\to(\psi\to\varphi)$,
  $(\varphi\to(\psi\to\chi))\to((\varphi\to\psi)\to(\varphi\to\chi))$, and
  $(\neg\varphi\to\neg\psi)\to(\psi\to\varphi)$;
\item \emph{reference}: only labels occurring in the stored Metamath proof.
  This provides an oracle ceiling for premise selection.
\end{itemize}

The same extracted assertion has a direct typed-rule form and a first-order
TPTP form.\footnote{TPTP is the standard problem language consumed by systems
such as E and Vampire.  TSTP extends that syntax with named inference steps and
parent links, representing the returned derivation as a replayable
DAG~\cite{tptp-derivations}.}  For example, \texttt{pm2.61ii} is represented
schematically as:

\begin{lstlisting}
; Direct Curry--Howard rule
(: pm2.61ii
   (-> (impl (neg $ph) (impl (neg $ps) $ch))
       (impl $ph $ch)
       (impl $ps $ch)
       $ch))
(chaining-rule pm2.61ii)

% First-order derivability encoding
fof(rule_pm2_61ii, axiom,
  ! [PH,PS,CH] :
    ((proved(imp(neg(PH),imp(neg(PS),CH)))
      & proved(imp(PH,CH))
      & proved(imp(PS,CH))) => proved(CH))).
\end{lstlisting}

In ordinary notation, \texttt{pm2.61ii} is the rule: from
$\vdash\neg\varphi\to(\neg\psi\to\chi)$, $\vdash\varphi\to\chi$, and
$\vdash\psi\to\chi$, infer $\vdash\chi$.
The first form asks for a proof term inhabiting the target type.  The second
turns derivability into a first-order predicate so that a saturation prover
can refute the axioms plus the negated target.  They express the same rule
application but expose very different search spaces.

\section{Direct chaining in Prime}

\subsection{Readable syntax and exact checking}

Prime's direct producer consumes ordinary typing declarations.  A rule joins
search only when explicitly marked; open declarations do not silently become
proof rules.

\begin{lstlisting}
(: ph Type)  (: ps Type)  (: ch Type)
(: h1 ph)
(: h2 ps)
(: h3 (impl ph (impl ps ch)))

(: ax-mp (-> $a (impl $a $b) $b))
(chaining-rule ax-mp)

!(search-first-inhabitant
    &self ch 3 100000
    (search-policy atp-guided-inhabitation))
; checked answer: (ax-mp h2 (ax-mp h1 h3)) : ch
\end{lstlisting}

In ordinary notation: from $\varphi$, $\psi$, and $\varphi\to(\psi\to\chi)$,
two modus-ponens steps derive $\chi$.
The producer instantiates a rule conclusion against the goal, creates its
premises as subgoals, and builds the application term.  Before returning a
candidate, the ordinary Prime typing path checks the term against the target.
The imported
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/CeTTa/blob/cetta-he-prime-dtt-chainer-20260715/lib/lib_atp.metta}
{\path{lib_atp} prototype} expresses the same direct construction using the
runtime's ordinary occurs-checked \texttt{unify}, together with \texttt{match}
and \texttt{check-type}.

The current performance lane is an explicit search policy, not checker
semantics.  Its data-free controls include head filtering, term and
formula-shape weights, premise cost, exact rigid-fact support, a 5:1
weight/age schedule, true fail-first premise ordering, and alpha-variant
ancestor regularity (no open goal may repeat an ancestor goal up to variable
renaming).  Every control changes discovery order only.

\subsection{Chronological selection closes the three-theorem frontier}

The oracle control is not the final selection result.  We also built a
deterministic selector whose training data for theorem $i$ end at theorem
$i-1$.  It starts with six recent assertions and the five assertions used most
often by earlier source proofs.  Because connective symbols alone cannot
distinguish this implication/negation corpus, it then performs one bounded
goal-directed relevance expansion.  Candidate conclusions are unified with
an open goal, and premise support is counted only when the premises join the
target hypotheses under one shared substitution.  Of at most four root
candidates, only those tying for the fewest unsupported premises may add one
earlier lemma whose instantiated premises are already target hypotheses.
These operations select a theory; they do not accept a theorem.

The selector is tested for chronological isolation.  Replacing the target's
stored proof, or replacing every proof at and after the target, must leave the
selected labels unchanged.  A cyclic selector substitution is also rejected.
Prime then performs its normal guided inhabitation search over the selected
theory, and a separate \texttt{check-type} call replays the returned term.
The
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_prime_chronological_tools.py}
{selector source},
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_prime_chronological_selection_v0.json}
{generated selection receipt}, and
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/run_setmm_prime_chronological_v0_gate.sh}

\section{Correctness bugs found by theorem proving}

Correctness falsifiers preceded performance attribution.  Two of them found
defects that ordinary happy-path tests had missed.

\subsection{Finite substitutions require an occurs check}

The first bug allowed an internal shape unifier to solve a type variable as a
term containing itself.  In the concrete regression, the rules below could
fabricate an inhabitant of an unrelated \texttt{Target} through a cyclic
equation equivalent to $X = \neg X$:

\begin{lstlisting}
(: pm2.24ii (-> $ph (neg $ph) $ps))
(: pm2.18i  (-> (impl (neg $ph) $ph) $ph))
(: notnotr  (impl (neg (neg $ph)) $ph))
(: notnot   (impl $ph (neg (neg $ph))))
\end{lstlisting}

In ordinary notation these rules are: from $\varphi$ and $\neg\varphi$ infer
$\psi$; from $\vdash\neg\varphi\to\varphi$ infer $\vdash\varphi$; and the
axioms $\neg\neg\varphi\to\varphi$ and $\varphi\to\neg\neg\varphi$.
The corrected path performs finite occurs-checked unification.  The suite now
requires all three outcomes:

\begin{lstlisting}
AcyclicSearchAccepted
CyclicSearchRejected
CyclicReplayRejected
\end{lstlisting}

It is not enough for the producer to reject the cycle: direct replay of the
pseudo-proof must also fail at the authority boundary.

\subsection{Unifiability is not tautology}

The native resolution prototype initially deleted any clause containing
opposite-polarity atoms that could unify.  That is unsound.  The clause
$P(X)\lor\neg P(Y)$ is not a tautology when $X$ and $Y$ are independently
quantified; $P(X)\lor\neg P(X)$ is.  The fixed deletion test requires exact
alpha-aware complement identity, not the existence of a unifier:

\begin{lstlisting}
(tautology ((ATP.Pos (p $x)) (ATP.Neg (p $x)))) ; True
(tautology ((ATP.Pos (p $x)) (ATP.Neg (p $y)))) ; False
(tautology ((ATP.Pos (p $x)) (ATP.Neg (p (f $x))))) ; False
\end{lstlisting}

This is why a large mathematical corpus is a correctness instrument as well
as a benchmark: a small local error eventually manufactures many false
theorems.  Both bugs now have positive controls, falsifying negatives, and
full-gate coverage.

\section{From E's TSTP refutation back to a Prime proof}

An SZS status is useful evidence but not a certificate.  The replay pipeline
therefore consumes E's actual TSTP proof for the full-prior theorem-182 problem.
Its
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_prime_e_frontier_tstp_replay_tools.py}
{TSTP-to-Prime generator} and
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/run_setmm_prime_external_tstp_replay_v0_gate.sh}
{live E/Vampire replay gate} are part of the public artifact.
The standard-library-only generator:

\begin{enumerate}
\item follows the empty clause's parent DAG;
\item treats E's clausified descendants as the resolution leaves;
\item standardizes variables, independently recomputes every binary unifier
  with an occurs check, and verifies the exact resolvent;
\item expands E's nested \texttt{spm} (paramodulation) and \texttt{sr}
  (simplify-reflect) records into explicit binary-resolution certificates;
\item carries proof holes through the refutation and extracts a direct Hilbert
  proof term; and
\item emits deterministic MeTTa replay data.
\end{enumerate}

The Python reader is only a generator.  It is not trusted logic.  Prime checks
each generated resolution claim, and the extracted term must separately pass
ordinary type checking.  A representative replay step has an explicit finite
substitution, selected pivots, two parent clauses, and the exact child:

\begin{lstlisting}
!(assertEqual
  (atp:resolution:check-step
    &left-parent &right-parent
    (ATP.Subst ((ATP.Bind x term-x) (ATP.Bind y term-y)))
    (ATP.Neg (proved (imp (ATP.Var x) (ATP.Var y))))
    (ATP.Pos (proved (imp term-x term-y)))
    &expected-child)
  True)
\end{lstlisting}

For \texttt{pm2.61iii}, the live E proof reproduces a frozen twelve-step
binary-resolution spine.  Prime accepts all twelve steps, rejects a forged
final resolvent, and checks the extracted term:

\begin{lstlisting}
(pm2.61ii
  (syl6com (con1d hypothesis_1) hypothesis_3)
  (pm2.61i pm2.27
    (a1d (con1i (con3i hypothesis_4))))
  hypothesis_2)
  : f_th_182
\end{lstlisting}

The proof uses eight prior rules---\texttt{pm2.61ii}, \texttt{syl6com},
\texttt{con1d}, \texttt{pm2.61i}, \texttt{pm2.27}, \texttt{a1d},
\texttt{con1i}, and \texttt{con3i}---plus the four theorem hypotheses.  It is
different from both the stored Metamath route and the small published support
set that motivated the frontier.  This is the desired ATP result: a new route
found in the full theory, with every accepted conclusion grounded in a direct
Prime proof.

The same pipeline replays E's four-step and Vampire's independently emitted
resolution proofs for \texttt{ja}.  Generator mutations alter pivots, parents,
or children; replay mutations alter substitutions or resolvents.  All are
required to fail.  Regeneration must be byte-for-byte deterministic, and live
E/Vampire output must reproduce the frozen fixtures.

\paragraph{Scope of the conformance claim.}
The current replay begins at the CNF descendants used by the refutation; it
does not claim a verified FOF-to-CNF transformation.  This does not weaken the
final theorem boundary because the reconstructed direct proof is checked
against the original Prime declarations.  A complete TSTP frontend should
eventually certificate-check clausification as its own transformation.

\section{Equality: ordered superposition as a second calculus}

Resolution is sufficient for the present \pcxp{} fragment because it has no
object-level equality.  Equality needs ordered rewriting or superposition.
The first equality rung uses the TPTP problem

\begin{lstlisting}
fof(eq_ab, axiom, a = b).
fof(p_a,   axiom, p(a)).
fof(goal,  conjecture, p(b)).
\end{lstlisting}

In ordinary notation: from $a=b$ and $P(a)$, conclude $P(b)$.
E reports the key inference as rewriting; Vampire exposes ordered
superposition.  Prime replays both as the same unit ordered-superposition
certificate: selected equality, direction, target literal, term position,
finite substitution, KBO (Knuth--Bendix ordering) configuration, and exact
child.  The configured KBO
orients $b \succ a$, so the certified rewrite is $b\to a$ inside the negated
goal.  Prime then resolves with $P(a)$ and checks the constructive equality
transport term.

Negative tests reject a reversed direction, changed child, changed position,
reversed precedence, cyclic substitution, and rewriting at an original
variable position.  This is real ordered equality replay, but only for the
unit-clause fragment.  It is not yet a full superposition saturation prover;
non-unit maximality, selection, factoring, simplification, and redundancy
remain future work.

\section{Native resolution in MeTTa: what works and what does not}

The imported ATP library now contains a transparent binary-resolution
producer written in MeTTa.  The
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/CeTTa/blob/cetta-he-prime-dtt-chainer-20260715/lib/atp/resolution_search.metta}
{resolution search library} and its
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/CeTTa/blob/cetta-he-prime-dtt-chainer-20260715/tests/prime/practical/atp_resolution_search.metta}
{positive and falsifying examples} are directly inspectable.  Its given-clause
loop (the standard saturation main loop) already has:

\begin{itemize}
\item ordinary occurs-checked MeTTa unification and standardization apart;
\item explicit set of support (inference stays anchored to goal-descended
  clauses);
\item bounded local generation and bounded global steps;
\item a 5:1 clause-weight/age schedule;
\item exact tautology and alpha-variant deletion;
\item a signed-literal Atomspace index; and
\item a replayable resolution proof tree or a bounded-incomplete receipt.
\end{itemize}

For example, the universally schematic clauses
$P(X)\lor Q(X)$, $\neg P(a)$, and $\neg Q(a)$ produce and replay the empty
clause.  The library also rejects a forged child and the cyclic unification of
$P(X)$ with $P(f(X))$.  Thus the inference rule, first-order variable handling,
proof production, and bounded-result algebra are operational.

The real-world full-prior result is negative.  Even the indexed native
resolution run for theorem 181 was terminated without a result after 119
seconds at 22.4 GiB peak resident memory.  The failure identifies the missing
standard stack precisely:

\begin{enumerate}
\item forward and backward subsumption rather than exact-variant deletion;
\item demodulation and ordered inference restrictions;
\item compact persistent clauses and proof DAGs rather than large interpreted
  proof trees on every queued item;
\item discrimination/substitution-tree-quality indexing; and
\item a resumable, shared-waiter agenda instead of retaining duplicated
  recursive continuations.
\end{enumerate}

These are classical base-prover mechanisms, not learned guidance.  A PLN,
neural ranker, or LLM should be measured only after this base is competent;
otherwise the experiment mostly measures avoidable redundancy.

\section{Architecture consequences for Prime}

The experiment suggests a small and durable boundary.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline]
\item[Prime authority]
Type elaboration, exact proof checking, subject/package identity, and replay of
explicit certificate steps.

\item[General MeTTa mechanism]
Ordinary match/unify/substitution, indexed Atomspaces, and eventually a
generic resumable stream/agenda handler with explicit bounds and ordering.

\item[Imported proof libraries]
Direct inhabitation, SLD/condensed detachment, resolution, superposition,
premise selection, and PLN/learned ranking.
\end{description}

Replay and discovery share the same rule-application semantics.  Replay pins
the rule, substitution, and parents; search leaves those choices open and must
schedule them.  This is the useful sense in which checking is the
fully-guided endpoint of proof search.  A MeTTa-native controller can therefore
rank ordinary proof-search choices and, later, PLN inference choices through
the same library interface, while neither ranking policy acquires authority to
accept its own result.

The present C \texttt{atp-guided-inhabitation} lane is useful as an explicit
performance and correctness probe, but its scoring coefficients are not
language semantics.  The pure-MeTTa \path{lib_atp} implementation demonstrates
the intended direction: proof search is readable, replaceable policy over
ordinary language operations; the checker remains unchanged.  If hot library
operations later need compilation, that is an implementation refinement, not
a reason to place one ATP calculus in the trusted core.

Direct inhabitation remains valuable: it constructs Curry--Howard proof terms
and is natural for Horn-shaped theorem rules.  Resolution remains valuable: it
has mature redundancy theory, a fixed empty-clause target, and direct
interoperability with E and Vampire.  Prime should host both and compare them,
not force one calculus to impersonate the other.

\section{An authentic quantified \texttt{set.mm} theorem}

The implication/negation frontier does not exercise Metamath's
distinct-variable (DV) side conditions.  We therefore added a separate source
replay for \texttt{ax5d}, which states
\[
\vdash\ \varphi\to(\psi\to\forall x\,\psi)
\qquad\text{provided $x$ does not occur in $\psi$,}
\]
where the proviso is exactly the source declaration \texttt{\$d x ps}.  Its
stored proof is two steps: the \texttt{ax-5} instance
$\vdash\psi\to\forall x\,\psi$, which carries the DV obligation, followed by
\texttt{a1i}, the rule inferring $\vdash\varphi\to\theta$ from
$\vdash\theta$.  In Metamath source form (\texttt{\$p} marks a provable
assertion, \texttt{\$=} its stored proof):

\begin{lstlisting}
${
  $d x ps $.
  ax5d $p |- ( ph -> ( ps -> A. x ps ) ) $=
    ( wal wi ax-5 a1i ) BBCDEABCFG $.
$}
\end{lstlisting}

The complete
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/run_setmm_prime_quantified_ax5d_v0_gate.sh}
{quantified replay-and-search gate},
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_quantified_ax5d_tools.py}
{source elaborator}, and
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_prime_quantified_ax5d_search_v0.metta}
{searched-term fixture} are available online.

The compact executable database is reconstructed from the pinned
\texttt{set.mm}.  Its compressed proof decodes to the reference sequence

\begin{lstlisting}
wps wps vx wal wi wph wps vx ax-5 a1i
\end{lstlisting}

and hence to the two-node logical DAG \texttt{ax-5; a1i} described above.
The grammar-driven (GSLT) source ledger---its Metamath grammar is supplied as
data to a generic parsing engine, not hard-coded---preserves the floating
hypotheses, scope, DV pair, theorem claim, and compressed payload.  Its
25-object semantic database agrees exactly with an independent run of
mm-lean4, a Lean~4-verified Metamath checker, before Prime sees the
assertion data.

\subsection{Search first, source semantics second}

The constructive producer is deliberately ordinary typed inhabitation.  It is
given the two relevant assertions, but no proof term and no trusted DV answer:

\begin{lstlisting}
(: Ax5dSearchVariable Type)
(: x Ax5dSearchVariable)
(: impl (-> Type Type Type))
(: all (-> Ax5dSearchVariable Type Type))
(: ph Type)  (: ps Type)

(: ax-5 (impl $formula (all $variable $formula)))
(type-scheme ax-5)
(: a1i (-> $formula (impl $antecedent $formula)))
(chaining-rule a1i)

!(search-first-inhabitant &self
   (impl ph (impl ps (all x ps))) 3 1000000)
; found: (a1i ax-5)
\end{lstlisting}

This search erases DV obligations only from the producer.  The exact returned
term is compiled into source applications of \texttt{ax-5} and \texttt{a1i}:

\begin{lstlisting}
(MMReplayApplyStep "ax5" "ax-5"
  (Cons (MMReplayBinding "ph" (ax5d:wff (ax5d:ps)))
    (Cons (MMReplayBinding "x" (ax5d:setvar "x")) Nil))
  Nil)

(MMReplayApplyStep "root" "a1i"
  (Cons (MMReplayBinding "ph" (ax5d:wff (ax5d:ax5-body)))
    (Cons (MMReplayBinding "ps" (ax5d:wff (ax5d:ph))) Nil))
  (Cons "ax5" Nil))
\end{lstlisting}

Replay does not trust those bindings.  It checks their typecodes and source
symbols, substitutes every mandatory hypothesis, compares the essential
parent, substitutes the conclusion, and recomputes each source DV obligation.
For \texttt{ax-5}, substituting $\varphi:=\psi$ and $x:=x$ turns the source
condition ``$x$ apart from $\varphi$'' (\texttt{\$d x ph}) into the target
obligation ``$x$ apart from $\psi$'' (\texttt{\$d x ps}).  The theorem frame
supplies exactly that pair.

This is Metamath's flat token-sequence substitution discipline, not a claim
that Prime now implements a general binder calculus.  The source checker makes
the quantified assertion sound by enforcing the same DV cross-product rule as
Metamath: every variable occurring in one substituted expression must be
distinct from, and declared disjoint from, every variable occurring in the
other.

The positive proof and ten falsifiers are executable.  Replay rejects a
missing target DV pair, capture producing \texttt{x/x}, a wrong quantified
variable, a changed parent, a changed substitution, a duplicate binding, and
an undeclared symbol.  It also rejects certificates that grant themselves an
extra target-frame DV assumption or an invented target hypothesis.  The producer bridge rejects a mutated
search term instead of manufacturing a certificate for it.  Thus the example demonstrates
quantified proof discovery followed by authentic Metamath checking, while
keeping search convenience outside the authority boundary.

\section{What the real-world test establishes}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prime can search, not only check.}  Full-prior direct
  search independently proves 181 and 183 and returns exact proof terms.
\item \textbf{Chronological premise discovery works at the frontier.}  A
  source-prefix-only selector reduces the three theories to 11, 11, and 12
  assertions; Prime searches and rechecks all three, including theorem 182.
\item \textbf{Prime can consume mature ATP output.}  Actual E and Vampire TSTP
  derivations replay through explicit resolution and ordered-equality checks.
\item \textbf{The hard frontier is now diagnostic.}  Theorem 182 fails in the
  full-prior direct producer but succeeds after chronological selection and is
  also solved by E in the same full theory; Prime checks both resulting proof
  routes.
\item \textbf{Correctness testing paid for itself.}  The corpus exposed an
  occurs-check bug and an unsound resolution deletion rule before either could
  contaminate performance claims.
\item \textbf{The boundary extends to a real quantified theorem.}  Prime finds
  the DV-erased \texttt{ax5d} proof term in its two-assertion selected theory,
  then source replay independently
  reconstructs substitution and enforces the authentic \texttt{\$d x ps}
  obligation.
\item \textbf{Native saturation is not competitive yet.}  Small MeTTa
  resolution is correct and readable; the full theory demonstrates that
  subsumption, simplification, compact proof sharing, indexing, and scheduling
  are load-bearing.
\end{enumerate}

The executable failures make the Prime ATP base useful for controlled
comparison.  Subsequent search policies can use the same CC0 problems,
E/Vampire oracle outcomes, and final checker, holding theorem, theory, and
acceptance constant.

\section{Reproduction}

Start from the
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/CeTTa/tree/cetta-he-prime-dtt-chainer-20260715}
{CeTTa Prime implementation branch} and the
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/tree/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification}
{Mettapedia reproducibility directory}.  The latter contains a
\href{https://github.com/zariuq/MeTTapedia/blob/prime-atp-pcxp-20260718/MettaKernel/kernel/pcxp_trace_certification/setmm_prime_atp_manifest.json}
{source-identity manifest}, the generated TPTP/TSTP corpus, and every command
named below.  GitHub renders each link as a browsable source page; the gates
regenerate and compare the executable data rather than relying on those
rendered pages.

The main executable checks are:

\begin{lstlisting}
# Prime correctness, cross-dialect, and mutation gates
make test-prime-all

# Regenerate Prime and TPTP problems from pinned set.mm
python3 setmm_prime_atp_tools.py \
  --setmm /path/to/set.mm \
  --source-revision 47e6e06b87581cd630d210dc41cf83b02eea78ea

# Live E + Vampire output, deterministic generators, Prime replay
CETTA=/path/to/cetta \
EPROVER=/path/to/eprover \
VAMPIRE=/path/to/vampire \
  ./run_setmm_prime_external_tstp_replay_v0_gate.sh

# Regenerate and check the 181 / 182 / 183 direct outcomes
CETTA=/path/to/cetta SETMM=/path/to/set.mm \
  ./run_setmm_prime_direct_frontier_v0_gate.sh

# Chronological selection, isolation mutations, search, and proof replay
CETTA=/path/to/cetta SETMM=/path/to/set.mm \
  ./run_setmm_prime_chronological_v0_gate.sh

# Quantified ax5d: source reconstruction, independent semantic agreement,
# DV-aware replay mutations, and DV-erased proof discovery
SETMM=/path/to/set.mm \
CETTA_PRIME=/path/to/cetta \
CETTA_GSLT=/path/to/gslt-enabled-cetta \
GSLT_GRAMMAR=/path/to/lib_parse_metamath_grammar_generated_v0.metta \
  ./run_setmm_prime_quantified_ax5d_v0_gate.sh
\end{lstlisting}

The external replay gate covers live E resolution for \texttt{ja}, live E
resolution for \texttt{pm2.61iii}, independent Vampire resolution for
\texttt{ja}, E rewriting, Vampire ordered superposition, deterministic
regeneration, extractor mutations, replay mutations, and final Prime proof
