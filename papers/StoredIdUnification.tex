\begin{abstract}
This note extends the named-shape-store theory with an executable stored-ID
unifier and semantic contracts prompted by concrete runtime defects. The
unifier retains terms as IDs, frames and lazy substitution closures; its
readback agrees with the existing finite-tree unifier. Soundness,
most-generality, finite completeness, logical rejection and exact resumption
are proved in Lean. Further results justify free-frame occurs-check omission,
shared skeleton alignment, contextual variable numbering, adaptive observers,
captured readers and pure guard normalization. A fault-injection experiment
also exposes a parallel-array capacity publication defect. Logical laws and
native implementation guarantees remain separate throughout.
\end{abstract}

\section{Retain information according to observations}

For state $s$, retained information $k(s)$ and an observer $o(s)$, sufficiency is
\[
        k(s)=k(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad o(s)=o(t).
\]
\code{PureGuardNormalForms.sufficient_factors} constructs an observer on the
reachable keys under exactly this condition. Conversely, equal retained keys
with different observable answers refute sufficiency. The observation may
include more than a returned value: guards, effect order, multiplicity,
completion status, live references and captured occurrence coordinates can
all distinguish states.

This is an observer-relative information criterion. It is not a proof of
globally optimal bit encoding, minimal allocation, or decidability of all
program equivalences. A larger observer family can require a larger key.

\section{Stored views and lazy substitutions}

A faithful accessor exposes a stored term's next constructor and child IDs.
Its source interpretation supplies the equation relating that single step to
finite syntax. The native implementation must establish this accessor
contract; the Lean development includes a concrete shared ID table as a
nontrivial instance.

A view has one of three forms:
\[
 v ::= \operatorname{cell}(f,x)\mid\operatorname{stored}(i,f)
       \mid\operatorname{subst}(c,v,w).
\]
Here $f$ is a variable frame. The same stored variable under two frames denotes
two distinct logic variables. Readback interprets the last constructor as
single substitution:
\[
 \llbracket\operatorname{subst}(c,v,w)\rrbracket
   =[c\mapsto\llbracket v\rrbracket]\llbracket w\rrbracket.
\]
Head exposure applies closures lazily: at a constructor it propagates them to
the child views, rather than materializing the decoded term. The occurs walk
uses this exposure operation. Its termination is proved by readback size,
but its operational body does not compute readback.

\code{StoredIdViews.head_correct}, \code{occurs_correct} and
\code{substitute_correct} establish the primitive simulation. The model uses
binary constructor syntax, with constants and scoped variables; it does not
claim a verified encoding of every native atom kind, list splice or grounded
equality implementation.

\section{The unifier and its completion boundary}

The worklist algorithm exposes both operands, deletes equal variables or
constants, decomposes aligned constructors, and installs lazy single
substitutions after the occurs check. Returned bindings remain views, not
decoded terms. For each allowance $n$,
\[
 \operatorname{solvedReadback}(\operatorname{run}(n,E))
     =\operatorname{unifyFuel}(n,\llbracket E\rrbracket).
\]
The two sides are independently recursive. The operational unifier never
calls the reference unifier or readback.

The correspondence yields soundness and most-generality of every solved
answer. Every solvable finite equation system succeeds with some finite
allowance. The result type distinguishes \emph{solved}, \emph{conflict} and
\emph{paused}. A paused result retains the exact pending equations and ordered
binding prefix. Splitting the allowance preserves the complete result:
\[
 \operatorname{run}(m+n,E)
    =\operatorname{resume}(n,\operatorname{run}(m,E)).
\]
Solved and conflicting outcomes persist with additional allowance. Combining
that persistence with finite completeness proves that conflict means no
finite-term unifier exists; it is not budget exhaustion.

This is an immutable lazy-substitution reference, not a model of the C
implementation's in-place binding cells. It proves no speedup or allocation
bound: closure creation, repeated head exposure and worklist size still cost
resources. Native mutation, allocation refusal and unsupported primitives
retain separate refinement obligations.

\section{Occurs-check omission and alignment}

The free-frame shortcut requires both:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the target variable belongs to another frame;
\item no active binding has a left-hand variable in the stored term's frame.
\end{enumerate}
The latter implies that the composed substitution fixes every variable of
that frame. The framed term therefore cannot acquire an occurrence of the
foreign target. A cached zero count justifies the shortcut only if it equals
the count of active bindings. Append and rollback preserve that invariant in
\code{StoredIdFrameConditions}; rollback restores the previous binding prefix
and all frame counts. Distinct frames alone are insufficient when a binding
redirects a variable into the target frame. An unbound frame alone is
insufficient when the target belongs to that same frame.

\code{StoredIdAlignment} computes one query view per skeleton hole. Successful
alignment has exactly the solutions of the full row equation, for every row
filling and every subsequent shared substitution. Thus one plan may be reused
across a read, while retaining the original frames and lazy closures. A plan
must not be reused after its captured prior bindings change.

The absence of an alignment certificate is not logical failure. In particular,
a variable facing a constructor, including a variable at an inner head
position, can unify with that constructor. The controls exhibit this case.
The typed row representation enforces hole arity; a native packed-array
adapter must enforce the corresponding boundary.

\section{Contextual alpha numbering}

Canonical variable numbers depend on the numbering context already accumulated
for the whole result. A known cell keeps its number; an unseen cell extends
the shared context. Numbering concatenated variable-occurrence streams agrees
with numbering the second stream under the first stream's outgoing context.

\code{ContextualAlphaNumbering.check_exact} relates a streaming validator of
stored ordinals to an independent number-producing traversal. A standalone
canonical part can fail this validation inside a caller context. For example,
after seeing cells $a,b$, the part $b,a$ must use ordinals $1,0$, not its
standalone ordinals $0,1$. Repeated parts retain repeated-variable relationships.
The result covers the variable-numbering stream, not native traversal coverage
or the full alpha codec.

\section{Storage observations and mutation}

The previous primitive bag refinement now lifts to finite programs whose next
operation depends on previous answers. \code{ShapeObserverPrograms} preserves
returned values, complete answer traces and final bags against an independent
all-rows interpreter. Membership is derived from logical count, not record
presence. Guards can observe earlier writes in the same pass, and duplicate
insertions into a bag remain duplicate occurrences.

That bag theorem is not enough for a cursor already in progress.
\code{CapturedRowReaders} distinguishes live revision validation from an owned
captured prefix. Detaching before publication preserves the remaining rows,
current position and subsequent occurrence results through arbitrary store
edits. A revision-only edit can otherwise invalidate a reader despite unchanged
row payloads. Epoch adoption is proved safe at the value level when the entire
captured prefix is unchanged; bag equality alone does not suffice. Native
index coordinates and pointer lifetimes need their own certificates.

An independent run of the saved S3g executable reproduced the shortest-path
\code{revision-invalidated} failure; the all-rows control completed with the
expected distances. The eager-mode dynamic-equation control also differed
from all rows: its final result retained \code{(lg 1)} rather than using the
added equation. The latter implementation diagnosis remains open; the model
does not label a particular cache as its confirmed cause.

\section{Wider guard regions through proved normalization}

Finite pure guard forms normalize to an ordered list of signed observations
and literals. Empty alternatives disappear; negations compose without a fixed
depth cap; metadata explicitly classified as inert disappears. Evaluation
preserves the complete ordered list of Boolean answers, including duplicates.
A singleton recognizer after normalization is therefore unchanged by an added
inert empty alternative, an inert annotation, or double negation.

The proof connects to the existing ordered-guard continuation semantics, so
replacing the pure provider preserves arbitrary consuming effects. Extraction
of that pure form remains an obligation of the active source profile.
An equation returning no answers can still perform effects while evaluating
its arguments. In the existing operational semantics an effect followed by
failure has no answers but changes the world. It is not removable as inert.
Likewise, a type declaration cannot be erased without establishing that it
does not change dispatch, argument behavior or failure in the selected profile.

These laws support wider regions than an equation-count or spelling test.
They do not implement a new CeTTa recognizer or claim maximal admission of
arbitrary semantically equivalent programs.

\section{Refusal must leave reusable state valid}

An allocator-failure probe against the inspected \code{term_molecule.c}
exposed an independent defect. When cell-array growth succeeds but parallel
frame-array growth fails, \code{mol_cell_of} returns decline with shared
capacity 32 and frame-array capacity 16. A retry writes the seventeenth frame
entry; AddressSanitizer reports a heap-buffer overflow. The probe injects one
allocation failure and includes the actual implementation source; it does not
modify the runtime worktree. This is failure-path evidence, not a claim that
ordinary workloads trigger the failure.

\code{PairedArrayAdmission} formalizes the required shared-bound invariant:
\[
 \operatorname{live}\le\operatorname{published}
 \le\min(\operatorname{valuesCapacity},\operatorname{framesCapacity}).
\]
Private allocations may grow independently. Publishing the shared bound only
after both successes preserves safe retry; refusal may retain a larger private
allocation without advertising inaccessible slots. The model proves safe
index admission and includes the failing $16\to32$ state as a counterexample.
It is not a C patch or a proof of native allocator behavior.

\section{Scope and qualification}

The nine new modules under \code{Mettapedia/Machines/} are
\code{StoredIdViews}, \code{StoredIdUnification},
\code{StoredIdFrameConditions}, \code{StoredIdAlignment},
\code{ShapeObserverPrograms}, \code{CapturedRowReaders},
\code{PureGuardNormalForms}, \code{ContextualAlphaNumbering} and
\code{PairedArrayAdmission}. \code{StoredIdOptimizationAudit} inventories their
module-owned mathematical declarations and transitive axiom dependencies.
No admissions, added axioms or native decision proofs are used. Compiler
runtime counterparts are inventoried separately from mathematical bodies.

The immediate native obligations are faithful stored-head access, shared
scopes and numbering, complete observer support, correct capture/publication,
failure-state validity and profile-qualified source normalization. Guard-aware
aggregation and effect batching require complete consumer support and the
authored order; no general commutativity or idempotence is assumed. The broader
transducer/CTL, decision-diagram, higher-derivative and optimal-cut packages
remain separate work.
