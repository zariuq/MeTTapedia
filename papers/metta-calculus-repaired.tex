\begin{abstract}
L.~G.~Meredith's \emph{The MeTTa calculus} proposes a symmetric,
reflective, higher-order interaction calculus~\cite{meredith}.
We retain its central idea of atomic unifying rendezvous and distinguish
that operation from passive storage, insertion, removal and observation.
The repair is expressed through the existing constraint multiset transform
rule, with physical occurrence identities and a common constraint store.
Lean proofs establish the store-operation distinctions, including a read
admission characterization for open terms and examples that bind caller
variables. A separate source counterexample identifies the problem with
the manuscript's literal identical store encodings. This is an initial
repaired variant, not a claim that the complete reflective language or its
translation has been qualified.
\end{abstract}

\section{Provenance and the scope of the repair}

The original calculus, symmetric communication rule, procedural reflection
proposal and discussion of Spaces as tags belong to Meredith~\cite{meredith}.
The inspected manuscript lists L.~G.~Meredith as its author and F1R3FLY.io
as the affiliation. We use the name \emph{MeTTa-calculus-repaired} for this
explicitly distinct proposal; it does not imply endorsement by the original
author. The original manuscript and its formal comparison owners are
preserved.

The design question is behavioral: can communication, querying and execution
use one transform mechanism while retaining the distinctions that programs
observe? Shared notation alone does not settle it. The original source and
this variant must remain independently identifiable. In particular, the
following critique is relative to a passive multiset store interpretation,
not an impossibility theorem about all encodings in the original calculus.

\section{A specific counterexample, and what it does not show}

Meredith's symmetric communication rule has the form
\[
 \frac{\sigma\in\operatorname{Unify}(t,u)}
 {\mathsf{for}(t\leftrightarrow s)P\mid
  \mathsf{for}(u\leftrightarrow s)Q
  \longrightarrow P\dot\sigma\mid Q\dot\sigma}.
\]
The manuscript's illustrative table uses the same expression
$\mathsf{for}(t\leftrightarrow s)0$ for insertion, removal and query.
With reflexive unification and the usual parallel unit law, this gives
\[
 \mathsf{for}(t\leftrightarrow s)0\mid
 \mathsf{for}(t\leftrightarrow s)0\longrightarrow 0.
\]
Two intended stored occurrences can therefore consume one another without
a store request. The Lean theorem
\code{Repaired.literal\_fact\_pair\_consumes\_both}
derives this through the independently specified recursive source relation,
using the existing executable unifier's reflexive case.

This counterexample rejects that literal passive-store interpretation.
It does not reject active symmetric rendezvous: two consenting participants
may intentionally consume their offers and continue. Nor does it exclude a
different storage protocol in the original calculus. The repair below makes
the intended distinction explicit instead of relying on an unstated protocol.

\section{The common transform rule}

An immutable label $\ell(o)$ belongs to each occurrence identity $o$.
Labels may contain open terms. A configuration $(C,W)$ consists of a finite
constraint store $C$ and a finite set $W$ of present identities. An already
freshened rule instance declares take patterns $t$, read patterns $r$,
equations $e$, checks $g$ and output labels $p$.

A selection chooses take, read and output lists $T,R,P$ in port order.
It forms one problem
\[
 C'=C\mathbin{+\!+}\operatorname{eq}(t,\ell(T))
       \mathbin{+\!+}\operatorname{eq}(r,\ell(R))
       \mathbin{+\!+}e
\]
and changes the present identities to
\[
 W'=(W\setminus\operatorname{set}(T))\cup\operatorname{set}(P).
\]
Takes are distinct and present; reads are present and may alias each other
but not a take. Outputs are distinct and fresh, with the declared labels.
Port lengths agree. The joint problem must have a solution, and each check
must hold in every solution at the original physical observation $W$.
Thus an inconsistent problem never authorizes a check by vacuity.

These are the existing \code{OccurrenceStep.Selection.Valid} and
\code{Selection.result} definitions. No additional transition primitive is
introduced by this repair. Operational rewriting changes occurrences;
structural equality is a separately declared relation on terms.

\section{Stored data and operations are different roles}

For this interpretation a label is $(\rho,s,v)$: a rigid role $\rho$,
a Space identity $s$, and a value $v$. Substitution acts on $v$ and fixes
$(\rho,s)$. Equal values in different Spaces or roles cannot unify as rows.
The role tags are ordinary data in this interpretation, not new operations
in the core calculus and not, by themselves, unforgeable permissions.

Write $D_s(t)=\data(s,t)$, $Q_s^m(t)=\req(m,s,t)$ and
$A_s^m(t)=\rep(m,s,t)$. Here $m$ is insert, erase or read.
Each invocation contributes one request occurrence. Three ordinary rule
schemas are
\begin{align*}
 \take\ Q_s^{\mathrm{insert}}(X)
   &\quad\longmapsto\quad
       \make\ D_s(X),A_s^{\mathrm{insert}}(X),\\
 \take\ Q_s^{\mathrm{erase}}(X),D_s(X)
   &\quad\longmapsto\quad
       \make\ A_s^{\mathrm{erase}}(X),\\
 \take\ Q_s^{\mathrm{read}}(X),\quad\readp\ D_s(X)
   &\quad\longmapsto\quad
       \make\ A_s^{\mathrm{read}}(X).
\end{align*}
Every use freshens its schematic locals; a request's actual caller variables
retain their declared sharing. The already freshened instance is what the
store-operation proofs take as input. This is one-witness querying. An
exhaustive answer family needs an additional coverage and answer-instance
construction; it is not obtained merely by repeating this equation.

\begin{theorem}[Open-term read admission]
Suppose invocation $q$ has label $Q_s^{\mathrm{read}}(u)$, datum $d$ has
label $D_s(v)$, and a candidate result $a$ has label
$A_s^{\mathrm{read}}(p)$. The selection $([q],[d],[a])$ is admitted exactly
when $q,d\in W$, $a\notin W$, and there exists one assignment $\sigma$
modeling $C$ for which
\[
       \sigma p =_E \sigma u =_E \sigma v.
\]
\end{theorem}
This is \code{Repaired.read\_admission\_iff}. The role equations imply
$q\ne d$. The common solution requirement preserves correlations between
the local pattern, the caller's request and the stored term. Independent
solutions to the two equations would not establish it.

\begin{theorem}[Store distinctions]
Every admitted store operation consumes an actual request occurrence.
Every admitted read requires and retains an actual data occurrence in its
declared Space. Every admitted erasure consumes an actual data occurrence.
A world consisting only of passive data cannot start these operation rules;
a world containing requests but no data cannot satisfy a read.
\end{theorem}
The generic theorem names are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{request\_required};
\item \code{read\_requires\_and\_keeps\_data};
\item \code{erase\_requires\_and\_consumes\_data};
\item \code{passive\_data\_do\_not\_supply\_requests};
\item \code{requests\_cannot\_answer\_an\_empty\_data\_store}.
\end{itemize}
They concern these rule schemas; deliberately installing another rule that
consumes data changes the declared behavior.

Concrete first-order controls start with a request containing a caller
variable and two distinct data identities both carrying $7$. Reading
retains both data identities and forces the caller variable to $7$ in every
solution. Erasing consumes exactly the chosen one. Inserting $7$ creates
a fresh third data identity. Two queries alone cannot furnish a datum.
The positive cases have actual valid selections; the controls do not pass
merely by refusing every interaction.

\section{Preserving symmetric rendezvous}

Meredith's useful active-peer interaction is retained as a separate
interpretation. Explicit party rows $\peer(s,t)$ and $\peer(s,u)$ are
consumed together; their continuation templates are produced under one
constraint store containing $t=u$. The theorem
\code{rendezvous\_uses\_one\_joint\_solution} establishes that every model
of an admitted interaction solves this equation. This retains symmetric
unification without treating passive data as active offers.

Continuation representation, contextual freshening and any conversion of
process-valued substitutions to names must be specified before claiming a
full source COMM correspondence. The generic template law alone is not
that correspondence. In particular, neither printed variable spelling nor
execution provenance supplies authority to change another caller's protected
joint observations or to install a rule.

\section{Binding, quotation and reflection contracts}

The following obligations are necessary for the complete repaired language.
They are not credited as completed by the store-operation theorems.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Binding.} State which pattern variables bind, which capture
caller variables, and how each activation obtains fresh locals. Renaming
must avoid all relevant free variables, including those in the pattern.
The manuscript's printed freshness condition on the continuation alone
does not specify this full contract. A complete account of alpha-equivalence
can also supply standardization apart; an omitted algorithmic instruction
alone is not proof that all original COMM derivations capture variables.
\item \textbf{Quotation.} Named code is inert under the declared execution
contexts; activation is explicit and preserves permitted lexical captures.
Syntax inspection and equality of meanings have different observations.
No unrestricted source-code inspection theorem follows from beta-equivalence.
The mixed \code{QuotedLambdaRetained} comparison now supplies finite paths
with actual incremental solver runs, preserving captures and retained
equations across beta reduction and activation. This is a lexical quotation
profile: it remains distinct from substitution-sealed rho names and does
not yet compose the complete repaired reflective language.
\item \textbf{Substitution and dereferencing.} Decide by a declared profile
whether activation is a separate step or is part of semantic communication
substitution. A missing standalone Drop rule is not by itself a defect if
semantic substitution supplies the intended behavior. For a staged encoding,
an enriched target observer may distinguish the intermediate suspended state.
\item \textbf{Procedural reflection.} Meredith's core REFL rule permits
one step derived from the same relation, including reflection. The
implementation discussion instead describes one COMM event. Both are
meaningful profiles. The Lean recursive relation admits a nested reflection
example that the existing COMM-only lookahead does not. Consuming a one-shot
reflection wrapper is not intrinsically erroneous.
\item \textbf{Authority and observations.} Ordinary labels do not authenticate
permissions. A complete construction must connect rule installation and
Space access to the declared permissions, retain the observation cut, and
separate speculative child execution from committed parent effects.
\end{enumerate}
The manuscript's deep-copy implementation is also separate from these
semantic obligations. No representation-independent copying lower bound
has been established here.

\section{A checked obstruction to an attractive communication shortcut}

A proposed two-parameter continuation would reconstruct a receiver from
$K[x,\mathsf{drop}(x)]$ and produce $K[\mathsf{quote}(Q),Q]$.
This does not uniquely determine the required output. Both
\[
 K_1(x,z)=z,\qquad K_2(x,z)=\mathsf{drop}(x)
\]
reconstruct the body $\mathsf{drop}(x)$, but their outputs are $Q$ and
$\mathsf{drop}(\mathsf{quote}(Q))$. The Lean construction in
\code{ContextualCommunicationControls}, namespace \code{SemanticPorts}, shows
that both assignments solve the actual selected transform problem and
that the rule is admitted. Its outputs remain different under the source
observation used by the intrinsic rho comparison.

Moreover, the proposed compound argument spine is outside the structural
pattern fragment. A certificate for a canonical factorization or an
explicitly represented two-binder receiver could repair this proposal.
Neither uniqueness nor solver completeness can be inferred from the
unrestricted equation alone. This obstruction concerns the proposed
encoding, not Meredith's original calculus or the impossibility of an
adequate communication construction.

\section{Proof map and remaining acceptance}

The formal owners are in
\code{Mettapedia.Languages.ProcessCalculi.MeTTaCalculus}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \code{Repaired}: ordinary rules, generic store laws and exact
open-term read admission; the literal source encoding counterexample.
\item \code{RepairedControls}: positive insertion, erasure and read,
caller binding, duplicate identities and query-only rejection.
\item \code{ReflectionClosure}: an independently parameterized recursive
source relation and the nested-reflection separator.
\end{itemize}
The arity-two counterexample has this owner:
\begin{quote}
\code{ConstraintMultisetRewriting}\\
\code{ContextualCommunicationControls}, namespace \code{SemanticPorts}.
\end{quote}
The shared core and contextual solver development remain their existing
owners, not copies introduced by this proposal.

The remaining acceptance is compositional: join scoped continuation
substitution, quotation and activation, selected reflection, Space
permissions and ordinary program execution; prove source preservation
and no invention for their declared observations. One-witness store access
must not be confused with exhaustive querying, and separate client
correspondences must not be confused with a mixed-language theorem.
