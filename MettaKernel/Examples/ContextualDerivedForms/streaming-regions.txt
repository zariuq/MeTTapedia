Streaming marker order and continuation association

StreamingRegions describes the existing agenda without adding a runtime
region table. Its markers projection retains kind, allocation key and, for a
collection, its exact saved caller. Marker keys strictly decrease through the
agenda and remain below nextBoundary. They are distinct across kinds.

framesFit links a saved continuation to later markers in the same order.
Source frames introduce no marker. A collection frame must find the marker
whose stored caller is exactly its own remaining continuation. A caller may
skip an old callee handler that remains pending after return. Requiring literal
equality between the frame stack and every older marker would reject that
valid case.

StreamingRegionSelections proves exact first/handler cuts and collection
association. Collection recording changes one bank and retains its key,
original substitution, caller, prefix and suffix. Ordered keys establish that
the marker found by the actual operation is the one required by the saved
frame. A well-linked collection, first frame or nearest handler earns success
of the actual finite marker search; marker presence is not simply assumed.
Generic first-selection construction/uniqueness laws remain with Data/List.

StreamingRegionPreservation covers every actual placement, delivery and
controller transition. Alternative expansion shares the older markers;
opening earns a fresh key; successful cuts retain a valid suffix; source phases
use the reached world without changing ownership of older jobs. The proof
imposes no termination requirement on the producer.

StreamingRegionReachability proves actual entry, arbitrary finite paths,
every bounded endpoint and paid resumption. Every pending task has its own
suffix-relative links, rather than borrowing the selected task's invariant.
Marker uniqueness and the fresh next key are consequences. These identities
are separate from the logical-variable allocation counter.

Negative controls distinguish missing markers, a matching number with the
wrong kind, duplicate keys across kinds and a collection with the wrong saved
caller. Positive controls retain an old callee handler outside the caller and
execute a first-demand cut that discards the second packet and inner handler
while preserving the outer collection. These are internal representation
controls, not new language admission restrictions or reported C defects.
