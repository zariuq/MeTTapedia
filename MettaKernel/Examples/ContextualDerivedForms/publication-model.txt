Prepared primitive publication: model and counterexamples
========================================================

Classification: derived model consumer and calibration expectations.

The Lean model is Machines/PreparedStoreProtocol. Its executable negative
controls are in PreparedStoreProtocolControls. The bridge in
ConstraintMultisetRewriting/Step/PublicationProtocol proves that a committed
model publication realizes an admitted existing ordered tcalc step.

Vocabulary and scope
--------------------
Key is a requested value key; O is a physical occurrence identity. Repeating
a requested key must not repeat the same physical deletion. Conversely, two
different occurrences carrying one key must remain two occurrences. In the
CeTTa exact-selection example, an AtomId is a value key, not an arbitrary
physical row position or occurrence lifetime.

World.space identifies the selected space. Plan.ticket identifies one
recorded preparation. Neither a space identity nor an operation kind alone
identifies that preparation. Natural-valued tickets model a serial issuer;
finite-word overflow and independent/forked issuers need their own contracts.

This model covers one serialized primitive publication. It does not impose
whole-transform rollback. Refusal preserves the current world, including any
effects performed by earlier primitives. Native allocation, memory layout,
pointer retention, actual locks and source-to-C correspondence are outside
the proved boundary.

Corrected contracts
-------------------
F1: record_exact and successful_receipt_taken_iff connect every receipt entry
    to the actual published footprint and immutable payload. Receipt.length
    is derived from constructed records; it cannot independently advertise
    more slots. completed_records_are_initialized checks the array-view law.

F2: duplicate_requests_irrelevant and ready_taken_distinct prove that request
    repetition does not duplicate a physical selection. The positive mixed
    case retains two equal-valued stored copies. removed_exact identifies
    the real before/after occurrence difference.

F3: rejected_prepare_preserves_state and busy_preserves_plan forbid changing
    an existing pending operation while reporting that preparation failed.
    A tagged operation carries its arguments together.

F4: mismatched_receipt_refused preserves both world and pending state when
    the supplied association is wrong. Publication constructs the receipt
    from its own plan; it accepts no foreign payload to certify. Recorded
    preparation advances the serial ticket supply; ordinary preparation does
    not advance it.

One-shot behavior: publication_consumes and cannot_republish prevent a
successful primitive from executing twice. Capacity refusal occurs before
publication and leaves both the store and ticket counter unchanged.

No compulsory second matching pass
---------------------------------
Ready states semantic dependency obligations. publishReady consumes existing
evidence and directly applies the physical effect. publish_under_ready proves
that the checked wrapper agrees with this direct path. The model therefore
does not require implementing preparation, validation and publication as
three separate scans.

no_recording_no_ticket_allocation proves that disabling recording leaves
the ticket supply unchanged. unrecorded proves that no receipt is constructed
for such a plan. These are model laws; physical allocation and cycle counts
still require implementation evidence.

Executable controls
-------------------
From the Lean project root:

  lake env lean --run ../../MettaKernel/Examples/ContextualDerivedForms/PublicationModel.lean

Compare stdout with PublicationModel.expected. The four deliberately broken
variants are expected to exhibit their named failures; the corrected protocol
has separate positive controls. This is not a test which changes its expected
answer to follow a native implementation.

The related finite store-program consumer is:

  lake env lean --run ../../MettaKernel/Examples/ContextualDerivedForms/RunStoreProgram.lean

It executes append/query/remove/query through the proved tcalc interpretation,
pauses after two operations and resumes without repeating the append. The
accounted charge and the protocol inspection budget are separate quantities.

This finite example uses an allocator fresh against the current live store.
FreshOccurrenceAllocation.Controls.deleted_identity_can_be_reused shows why
retained historical cursors need a stronger supply or a wider avoidance set.
The example does not select an allocation policy for Prime or CeTTa.

Refinement limits
-----------------
The erase readiness profile observes the complete requested key region at
publication. A native captured-prefix deletion can have narrower dependencies
and deliberately leave later matching appends alone. Do not apply the model's
live-region check to that different contract. The four defect controls keep
the store stable between preparation and publication, so their conclusions
do not depend on choosing one of these observation policies.

An append here is one prepared atomic append effect. CeTTa's ordered
ADD_BATCH helpers may represent several source operations and retain an
earlier prefix on later failure; those helpers must be compared compositionally,
not declared equivalent to one atomic append merely because both use a batch.

The reference's rejected preparation preserves its entire State. The essential
runtime rejection contract is preservation of the logical world and pending
operation. A failed recorded preparation may consume an unused ticket or
profiling work without violating that contract, provided association remains
fresh and no successful receipt is fabricated. Consecutive numerical ticket
values are not a required language observation. The ordinary no-recording path
still must not pay for receipt-only ticket allocation.
