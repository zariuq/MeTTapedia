# Higher-order MeTTa: profiles, assemblies and comparisons

This directory studies ways to combine higher-order mathematics, dependent
judgments and reflective execution. Each result concerns the calculus, model,
operations and observation named in its statement. The directory groups those
constructions; it is not an exhaustive enumeration of possible Prime systems.

## Reading a claim

A universal theorem quantifies over its actual mathematical parameters: for
example a `Language.Model`, a validated operation signature, a displayed
family, or a normalized transition system. An existential assembly supplies
one compatible choice of components at the same index. A counterexample rules
out the stated implication or observation erasure. These claims have different
strengths; their quantifiers are not supplied by a folder name.

The specification index records one scoped-identity development direction and
five unselected decision families (`identity_direction_selected`,
`unselected_families`). Recorded decisions and the option graph give finer
information than this six-family summary. The sharing study narrows its own
four-policy class to per-source and per-candidate policies under ordered answer
observation; it does not choose a complete language.

## Grounding and common mathematics

Independent source kernels retain their own syntax and checker theorems.
Optional translations into HOL, logical frameworks or dependent profiles state
preservation, reflection and their admitted images separately. A theorem about
a source checker, a native execution receipt and whole-system qualification are
separate evidence. HOTG remains an optional interpretation profile.

Shared path transport lives in `GSLT.Core.InteractionComposition` and
`GSLT.Core.OperationalPathFibration`; linear resources live in
`GSLT.Causality.ResourceInteraction`. Shared evidence and its observation laws
live in `TypeTheory.DisplayedEvidence`; Need execution and probabilistic
resumption live in `Machines.BranchLocalNeed` and `GSLT.Dynamics`.
Candidate adapters use these constructions rather than defining another copy.

Module paths follow the subject grouping. Established mathematical declaration
names remain stable on folder-only moves; changes of mathematical home have
explicit declaration migrations. Collection coverage and dependency direction
are checked by the existing Prime and type-theory boundary scripts.
